The speaker uses a forex strength matrix to compare GBP, EUR, USD, JPY, CHF, CAD, and AUD, looking for strong/weak currency confirmation. The London morning is emphasized because of volume and the Cable morning trade after a small Eastern session. No explicit stop, target, RR, or news rule is stated.

## Video 2: Live Trading GBPUSD Making $6000 With This Simple Strategy

Daily determines the main trend. 4H maps structure, momentum, and higher-timeframe supply/demand points of interest. M5 is the normal execution timeframe; M15 may be used when the structure is the same and clearer.

The strategy waits for price to tap a higher-timeframe POI and then create a lower-timeframe structural shift. Examples include printing lower lows/lower highs into demand and then shifting to create a higher high. After the shift, a lower-timeframe imbalance demand/supply zone is created, and the entry is a limit order on that new zone. Random limit orders at a higher-timeframe POI are explicitly rejected.

Liquidity examples include consolidation lows, the Asia low, and the previous day low. Momentum should be slow and corrective on the pullback; strong opposing intent is unfavorable. DXY’s inverse correlation with GBP/USD is used as confluence, with a GBP/USD BUY ideally supported by falling DXY structure and liquidity behavior.

The stop is where the trade thesis is invalidated. The target is mechanically fixed at 1:3 RR, and the strategy intentionally avoids targeting many different structural highs/lows or liquidity pools because that creates inconsistency. It uses set-and-forget management: hold to SL or TP, with no break-even and no partials. London is mentioned as contextual timing. The strategy avoids very clear impulsive momentum against the setup and does not place an order at a distant origin after a massive impulse when a closer adaptive zone is needed.

## Video 3: How To Trade GBPUSD - My London Session Strategy Explained

The video uses the 15M chart. It identifies a London High and London Low. A body close above London High creates a BUY bias; a body close below London Low creates a SELL bias. Wicks are ignored for BOS; a valid break requires the candle body to close beyond the prior structure body.

After BOS, the strategy does not enter at the new high or low. It waits for a deep retracement and enters during the retracement, potentially with a limit order. The preferred tested RR is fixed at 1:3. The example uses a 10-pip stop and 30-pip target, but the video warns against treating distant previous highs/lows or huge targets as automatic objectives because they reduce the chance of completion within the London session.

The strategy is checked during the London morning. A setup may use an alert or pending order. If there is no retracement, the trade is not chased. If an alert or pending order never triggers, the trader closes the charts for the day.
